# SwoopSwap: Project Proposal

**Team:** Danny Chen, Ruilin Chen, Leah Loukedis, Chloe Peyrebrune, Coralynn Yang, Sihao Zhang
**Product Owner:** Chloe Peyrebrune · **Scrum Master:** Coralynn Yang

## Narrative

Every May, Emory's dumpsters fill with usable mini-fridges, chairs, and lamps, and a few months later incoming students buy the same things new. SwoopSwap is a secondhand marketplace for Emory students only: accounts are verified through Emory email, so everyone buying or selling is a fellow student. Sellers list items for sale or for free, and buyers message them to arrange a pickup. Later epics add buyer and seller ratings, Atlanta-area sublets, and student-offered services.

**Sustainability.** Every resold item stays out of a landfill and replaces a new purchase, and the app will show an estimate of waste diverted so that impact is visible. Secondhand prices also lower move-in costs for students with less money. We also plan to keep the app lightweight so it uses few resources outside the busy move-in and move-out periods.

## Project Micro-Charter

**Code name:** SwoopSwap

**Mission:** Help Emory students pass along what they no longer need, so less of it ends up in the trash.

**Vision:** A campus where move-out week produces a marketplace instead of a dumpster pile.

**Elevator pitch:** SwoopSwap lets Emory students buy, sell, or give away dorm essentials to other verified students, organized around move-out dates.

**Business value:** Students leaving recover money, students arriving save it, and Emory cuts move-out waste.

**Customers:** Emory students moving out (sellers) and moving in (buyers); possibly Residence Life or the Office of Sustainability as a partner.

**Metrics:** Listings completed per semester, estimated pounds of waste diverted, and time from listing to sale.

**Milestones:** Core marketplace first, then ratings and sublets, then polish and deployment before the Dec 9 Showcase.

**Resources:** Six students; a web app built with Next.js, Supabase, and Vercel, using free tiers.

**Risks:** Too few early listings, activity that only spikes at semester boundaries, meetup safety, and scope creep from the extension epics.

**Trade-offs (fixed to fluid):** Dates are fixed, quality is firm, resources are flexible, and scope is fluid, so we cut features before we cut quality.
